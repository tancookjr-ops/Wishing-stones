The Door & The Box

People will happily pay for a great night out at Wishing Stones — more happily than we tend to ask. This is a way to keep the door open to everyone, hand every artist their box, and still come out the far side with real money in the tin. The box stays. The door opens.

the two promises

Everything else on this page is only allowed to exist because these two never break.

Nobody is ever turned away.

There is always a pay-what-you-can door — from zero, no questions, no awkwardness. An islander on a thin week gets the same seat as anyone. This is the soul of the place, and it is why people trust us enough to pay more when they can.

The artist's box is always the artist's.

The tip box by the stage stays exactly as it is — every coin the artist's, untouched. What changes is that we stop confusing the box with the door. The door covers the building and the night. The box thanks the artist. Two different things.

01Stop leaning on one box. Build a stack.

Right now the whole night rests on one box of loose change — and that box belongs to the artist anyway, so the gallery keeps nothing. The fix isn't to charge more in one place. It's to earn a little in several places that stack up. Read it bottom to top: the dark layers quietly cover the costs, so everything above them is profit.

Grants and memberships arrive before the event and cover the artist's fee, the heat, and Hilary's time. They turn the night's fixed costs into a solved problem.

Everything above the floor is profit.

Once the floor covers the costs, the door, the café, the shop and the money-moments aren't recovering losses — they're building the surplus that keeps Wishing Stones going.

02Choose your seat, not your guilt.

Pure pay-what-you-want quietly asks everyone to pay the least they can. This does the opposite: it names a fair price, offers a generous one for those who want to give more, and still keeps a real seat for anyone who can't. Same room, same night — people simply choose the tier that fits them. Most visitors, given the choice, don't pick the bottom.

the ferry runs the register

Two clocks: the day boat and the Friday boat.

The ferry decides who can spend money and when. Fight it and you lose; program to it and it pays. Day-trippers land at 11 and must be gone by 3:30 — a big, hurried, cash-in-pocket crowd with nowhere else to go. Only the Friday boat (and later weekend runs) lets a visitor stay for a proper evening. So we run two different businesses on the same floor.

03Build little money-moments into the night.

These aren't add-ons that cheapen the evening — done with a light touch, they're the most fun part of it. A room that's already warm and generous will happily throw a few more dollars at something playful, especially when the cause is the very place they're standing in.

The 50/50 draw

Sell tickets through the interval; the pot splits in half — one half to the winner, one half to the gallery. Maritime crowds already know the ritual, and nobody minds because they might win.

a $280 pot → $140 to the tin

The silent auction

One donated piece from a local artist sits on the wall all evening with a bid sheet beside it. Bidding closes at the last song. The artist gets the exposure and a receipt; the gallery keeps the hammer price.

one donated painting → $200+

The lot of the night

A single live-auction item the room can rally around — a private house concert, a portrait commission, a signed print of Oleander. One good-humoured minute, real money.

one lot → $100–500

The gallery basket raffle

Bundle a few slow-moving shop goods into a basket and raffle it by the door. It clears stock, seeds impulse buys, and every ticket is pure margin.

40 tickets at $5 → $200

04The gallery is the last room they walk through.

An audience that's just had a moving evening is in the most generous mood it will be in all week. Don't send them straight out the door — send them out through the art. This is where a good night quietly becomes a profitable one.

  • Lead with your own photography.

    The boats, the harbour, Oleander. It's the highest-margin thing on the shelves and it's uniquely yours — the print nobody else can sell.

  • Champion the local makers.

    Their work on consignment turns their fans into your foot traffic, and every sale takes a margin while supporting the island's artists.

  • Make the "night-of" bundle.

    A ticket + a small print, or a workshop + the thing they made, sold as one. Bundles lift the average sale without feeling like a hard sell.

the Oleander proof

People buy the story, not just the print.

Jason Mraz once flipped through the binder and stopped cold — "is that Oleander?" — at a photo of the bow, the rope, the mooring. He bought it on the spot.

That's the whole cross-sell in one moment: the picture carried a story he recognised, and the story is what he paid for. Every visitor who's just spent an evening in this building is holding a little of that same feeling. Give them something to take home.

05The quiet money that arrives before the doors open.

Ticket nights are the show. But the money that makes the whole thing safe is the money that's already in the account before anyone shows up — and it's the same money that unlocks the bigger nights.

Friends of Wishing Stones

An annual membership. It turns the ~100 islanders and the repeat cottagers into a dependable base — and lets the community fund the community, which is exactly how they want it.

  • Individual, per year $40
  • Household, per year $75
  • Free island-only nights + 10% shop always

Arts grants

Grants exist to cover the artist fees and the fixed costs — the "loss-leader" part of a night. A documented, well-attended, ticketed program is exactly what makes you competitive for them.

  • Canada Council for the Arts project
  • Arts Nova Scotia / Support4Culture annual
  • District of Chester community grant local

Summer camping — the unlock

There's no overnight accommodation, and that caps every evening event at islanders plus the late boat. A simple summer campground earns its own keep and lets visitors stay for the flagship nights and the retreats.

  • Tent site, per night $30
  • Unlocks weekend retreats $150+/head

what changes on Monday

Five things to try before the summer boats.

  1. Split the door from the box.

    Put out a tiered "choose your seat" sign and a separate, clearly-labelled artist's box. Say it out loud at the start of the night.

  2. Name a Standard price.

    Pick one honest number — $20–25 — and let people trade up to Patron or down to Neighbour. Watch how few pick the bottom.

  3. Open "Friends" memberships.

    A single card and a jar. Sell the first ones to the islanders who already pour money in — they'll be glad of a formal way to help.

  4. Add one money-moment.

    Start with the 50/50 draw — it's the easiest, the most familiar, and the least like selling. Build to the silent auction later.

  5. Route the exit through the shop.

    Move your own photography to eye level by the door, and mention one print from the stage. Let the good mood do the rest.

keep the door open — and make it pay.
